Time series and forecasting

This is the picture behind Session 15. A time series is just data that arrives in order, one number after another, and forecasting is guessing the next few numbers from the ones you already have. Read this once and the session will feel easy. No proofs here, only the idea.

What a time series is

A time series is a column of numbers recorded at regular times, each with a date attached:

  • the Nifty 50 closing value each trading day,
  • units of a product sold each month,
  • electricity load each hour,
  • rainfall each week.

The one thing that makes it special is order. The numbers come in sequence, and each one usually looks a lot like the one just before it. That is what lets you forecast at all: the near future tends to resemble the recent past.

Most series are three simple things added together

A surprisingly good way to think about almost any business series is as a sum of three parts:

  • a trend, a slow drift up or down (a brand growing year on year),
  • a season, a pattern that repeats every fixed number of steps (air conditioners sell more every summer, so a 12-month cycle),
  • noise, the random wobble you cannot explain and should not try to.

If you can see which of these are present, you already know a lot about which forecasting method to reach for. A method needs a component for every structure you can see: no season in the data means no seasonal term in the model.

Stationary, and why we "difference"

A series is called stationary when its average and its spread stay roughly the same over time. A flat, wobbly series is stationary. A steadily rising one is not, because its average keeps climbing.

Many forecasting tools want a stationary series to work on. The usual fix is differencing: instead of the value itself, look at the change from one step to the next. A rising trend becomes a roughly flat series of changes. There is a quick test, the ADF test, that gives a small number (a p-value below 0.05) when a series looks stationary. This is exactly the "make it flat first" step in the session.

"Today leans on the recent past": AR and MA in words

The classic model, ARIMA, is built from two plain ideas, and both are just a weighted average over time:

  • AR (autoregressive) says today is a weighted sum of recent values. Today leans on yesterday and the day before. Think of it as momentum.
  • MA (moving average) says today is a weighted sum of recent shocks, the random surprises. Think of it as a fading echo of the last few surprises.

ARIMA(p, d, q) is only three small whole numbers: how many past values to lean on (p), how many times you differenced to get stationary (d), and how many past shocks to lean on (q). ARIMA(1, 1, 1) means "difference once, use one past value and one past shock". That is the whole model.

The other family: exponential smoothing

Exponential smoothing reaches the same "weighted average of the past" from a gentler direction. It gives the most recent point the most weight and lets older points count a little less each step, so the weights fade smoothly into the past. One knob controls how fast the memory fades. Its three members grow with your data: simple smoothing tracks a level, Holt adds a trend, and Holt-Winters adds a season. It needs no stationarity check and is often a surprisingly strong baseline.

A forecast is a fan, not a line

The single most useful habit in this session: never report a forecast as one number. The future is uncertain, and the further ahead you look the less you know, so a good forecast comes with a prediction interval, a band that widens as it goes. A shopkeeper who plans for "about 100, and quite possibly 80 to 120" makes better decisions than one who plans for "exactly 100". The band is the part that carries the honesty.

Why industry cares

Forecasting is one of the most-asked-for skills in any analytics or finance job. How many units to stock next week, where demand or server load is heading, what a price might do next. The two workhorses here, ARIMA and exponential smoothing, are the honest baselines every forecaster starts from before reaching for anything fancier.
