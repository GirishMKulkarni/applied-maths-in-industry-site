Optimization projects

These three projects use only what we did in the optimization module, Sessions 20 to 24. Linear programming, yes/no decisions with integer programming, convex problems in cvxpy, simulation with SimPy, inventory and Markov chains. Nothing new to learn.

Each project is small. There is one person with a real problem and three questions in that person's own words. All the numbers you need are in the brief itself, so there is nothing to download. The code you need is already in your class notebooks. Your job is to write the problem in maths, solve it, check the answer, and explain it to someone who has never heard of a solver.

How this works

  1. Work in your project team. Each team picks one of the three projects below.
  2. Read your brief fully before you write any code.
  3. For every question, first write the problem in words: what you decide, what you want to make as big or as small as possible, and what rules must be followed. Then write the code.
  4. If you do not know where to start a question, open the help box in your brief. It tells you which session and which notebook to start from.
  5. Write a short note for the person in your brief. Two pages, plain words, numbers in rupees, no code.

You have about two weeks. It is marked out of 50.

Choose one

O1 What to Make from the Milk A village milk co-operative · Production planning O2 The Fertiliser Money A farmer in Vidarbha · Budget allocation O3 The Chemist Shop A medical store owner · Queues, stock and customers

What you hand in

  1. A GitHub repo with your notebooks. Every notebook must run from top to bottom on Google Colab. Fix the random seed so we get the same numbers as you.
  2. A note of two pages for the person in your brief. Tell them what to do, how much money it makes or saves, and what could go wrong. If the note needs a formula or a line of code to make sense, rewrite it.
  3. A five-minute talk in class. Show one chart or one table and give your answer to each of the three questions.

Three rules for all three projects

Read the solver's status first. Before you look at any answer, print the status. "Optimal" means the answer can be used. "Infeasible" means your rules cannot all be true at once, and the numbers printed after it mean nothing.

Check the answer by hand. After every solve, take the answer and check each rule yourself, the way we did with the thali in Session 20. Is the milk used less than the milk available? Does the money add up to the budget? A wrong sign in one constraint gives a wrong answer that looks perfectly fine.

Talk in rupees. "The shadow price is 4.5" means nothing to the people in these briefs. "One more hour of work earns you ₹4.50 more" means something.

How it is marked

Out of 50.

Marks What gets the marks
Question 1 12 The problem written clearly in words and in maths, correct code, the answer checked by hand and stated in one plain sentence
Question 2 12 Same
Question 3 12 Same
The note 10 Two pages that the person in your brief could read and act on, with rupee numbers and one honest paragraph on what could go wrong
The notebooks run 4 We open them on Colab and run everything. They finish without any help from us

We do not mark you on finding a big number. If your answer is "do not buy the machine" or "do not take the loan", and you show clearly why, that can get full marks.
