Start here — welcome to the course

Read this once before Week 1. It takes about fifteen minutes and it will save you a lot of worry later.

This course is for you, whatever you studied

People in this room come from many streams: mathematics, commerce, economics, computer science, engineering, statistics, and more. Some of you have written a lot of code and some of you have never written a line. Some of you love proofs and some of you last saw algebra a few years ago.

All of that is fine. The course is built so that every one of you can follow the main path, and so that you can pick up anything you are missing on the side, without holding up the class. You do not need to be a programmer to start, and you do not need a mathematics degree to keep up.

The promise is simple. By the end you will be able to take a real business problem, turn it into something a computer can solve, and explain the answer to a non-technical person. That is a skill Indian industry pays well for, and it does not belong to any one degree.

How every session is built

Each session moves through the same four steps. Knowing this makes the material much easier to follow.

  1. The problem. We start with a real situation. A food-delivery app guessing your delivery time. A bank deciding whom to give a loan. A warehouse near a metro deciding how much stock to keep. Something you can picture.
  2. The one idea. Then we say, in plain English and with a picture, the single idea that solves it. No symbols required to understand the sentence.
  3. The build. Then we write the code that does it, step by step, running and seeing the result at every stage.
  4. Go deeper (optional). At the end there is a box for the formal mathematics, marked optional. If you love maths, this is where the real depth is. If you do not, you can skip it and you will still have done real work.

When a word or an idea is new to you, do not panic and do not fall behind. Two things are always there for you:

  • Primers in the primers/ folder: short, picture-first notes that explain one idea in about ten minutes, with no proofs.
  • A glossary at primers/glossary.md: the plain meaning of every technical word we use.

In the notebooks you will also see cells marked Stretch (optional). Those are for people who already code or already know the maths and want to go further. If that is not you yet, skip them with a clear conscience.

A quick, honest self-check

This is only for you. Nobody sees it. Its job is to tell you where to spend an hour before Week 1 so the course feels smooth instead of scary.

Part A — comfort with code. Give yourself one point for each "yes":

  • I have installed something like Python or run code before.
  • I know what a variable is (a name that holds a value).
  • I have used a loop to repeat something.
  • I could write a few lines to add up the numbers from 1 to 100.
  • I have used Google Colab, Jupyter, or a similar notebook.

Part B — comfort with the maths ideas. One point for each "yes":

  • I am comfortable with averages, percentages and simple graphs.
  • I have seen the equation of a straight line, y = m·x + c.
  • I know what a probability of 0.7 means.
  • I have met the idea of a matrix or a table of numbers.
  • I have seen the words "mean" and "spread" (or "standard deviation").

Now read your two scores.

Your scores What to do before Week 1
Code 0–2 Do the fifteen-minute primers/python_in_15_minutes.md and run its cells. That is enough to start.
Code 3–5 You are ready on the code side. Look for the Stretch cells to stay engaged.

Most people are strong on one side and light on the other. That is exactly the room this course is designed for. Being light on one side is not a problem, it is just information about where to spend a little time.

Setting up (the five-minute version)

You have two ways to run everything. You do not need both.

The easy way — Google Colab (nothing to install). Every notebook has an "Open in Colab" button on the course website. It opens in your browser and runs on Google's computers for free. If you are new to code, use this. You need only a Google account.

The own-machine way (optional). If you would rather run things on your own laptop, install Python with uv and the course libraries once. The instructor will walk through this for anyone who wants it. It is not required to keep up.

Your first tiny success

Open the notebook 00_start_here/notebooks/hello.ipynb (or its Colab button) and press the play button on each cell. You will print a message, do a little sum, and draw your first chart. That is it. If those cells run, you are fully set up for the whole course.

Welcome aboard. See you in Week 1.
